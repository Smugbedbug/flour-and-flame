# File 2: Illinois cottage food rules, labels, selling, payments and business setup

Researched November 20, 2026 from official sources (listed at the end). Laws and guidance change: check the official source for anything important, and tell the owner who to confirm with. You are not a lawyer or accountant. Give the practical answer, cite the source and name the right office.

**Owner's situation:** home bakery in Plainfield, Illinois (most of Plainfield is in Will County; part is in Kendall County, so she registers with the health department of the county she lives in). Her cottage food registration is planned to start **January 2027**. She has no payment accounts yet. She may form a business (LLC) later but hasn't decided.

---

## 1. Cottage food basics (410 ILCS 625/4, the Home-to-Market Act, plus the 2026 Cottage Food Guide and its May 2026 addendum)

- **What it is:** food made in the kitchen of her primary home and sold **directly** to consumers by her, a family member or an employee.
- **Registration:** with her **local health department** (Will County Health Department, 815-727-8480: Application for Cottage Food Operations Registration plus the Cottage Food Home Self-Certification Checklist). **Annual.** The fee can't exceed **$50**. A copy of the certificate must be available on request. Registration covers **one home kitchen**. Once registered, she can sell anywhere in Illinois.
- **Training:** anyone who prepares or packages the products needs an **ANSI-accredited Certified Food Protection Manager (CFPM)** certificate. Some county Extension offices offer the course.
- **Inspections:** only on triggers such as a complaint, an outbreak, an imminent hazard or suspected mislabeling. The health department can inspect, charge a reasonable fee, stop sales and, if a problem isn't fixed, revoke the registration.
- **Not allowed in a licensed commercial kitchen:** cottage foods can't be made there. A separate retail license can exist alongside, but the products must be kept clearly separate.

## 2. What she can and can't make

**Prohibited** (relevant to a bakery): meat, poultry, fish; **dairy, except** as an ingredient in a non-potentially hazardous baked good or candy (e.g. caramel) **or in frosting such as buttercream**; **eggs, except** in a non-potentially hazardous baked good or in buttercream-type frosting **when the eggs are not raw** (the 2026 addendum says this excludes *pasteurized raw* eggs too; meringue powder is fine); pumpkin and sweet potato pies, cheesecakes, custard and cream pies, and **pastries with potentially hazardous fillings or toppings**.

**Baked goods with cheese:** the health department may require a lab test at her expense. Water activity must be ≤ 0.85, and an approved recipe must keep the same cheese variety or brand.

**Frosting guidance (May 2026 addendum):**
- dairy such as butter and cream is allowed in frosting
- no raw or pasteurized raw eggs
- health departments *may* ask for a water activity test; the law doesn't require one
- if a frosting can't be shown to be shelf-stable, the health department may require it to be kept, transported and sold below 41°F, which a home pickup bakery can't realistically do
- many cream cheese and whipped cream frostings should be kept below 41°F
- when unsure, treat a frosting as TCS (needs refrigeration) and test it

**Lab thresholds** (to prove a food doesn't need refrigeration): water activity < 0.88 **or** pH < 4.2 if using one measure. If neither is clearly met, the health department may ask for both.

### Decision table for cupcake frostings and fillings

| Item | Rating | Why |
|---|---|---|
| American buttercream (butter or shortening + powdered sugar, little liquid) | **ALLOWED** | Named in the law; high sugar = low water activity. The health department may still ask for a test. |
| Royal icing / buttercream made with meringue powder | **ALLOWED** | Dehydrated eggs are allowed. |
| Simple powdered-sugar glaze | **ALLOWED** | Very high sugar. |
| Firm cooked caramel / toffee pieces (cooked to candy stage) | **ALLOWED** | Dairy allowed in candy like caramel. |
| Soft caramel sauce or drizzle with cream | **ASK THE HEALTH DEPARTMENT** | More moisture; may need a water activity test. |
| Swiss or Italian meringue buttercream (egg whites heated/cooked) | **ASK THE HEALTH DEPARTMENT** | Eggs must not be raw, and pasteurized raw is excluded. Cooked whites may qualify; get it in writing. A meringue-powder version avoids the question. |
| Chocolate ganache (chocolate + cream) | **ASK THE HEALTH DEPARTMENT** | Not addressed in the guidance; depends on the ratio. Higher chocolate ratios are firmer and drier. Expect a water activity test. |
| Ermine / flour (cooked milk pudding) buttercream | **ASK THE HEALTH DEPARTMENT** | High moisture; likely TCS. |
| Shelf-stable jam filling (high sugar) | **ALLOWED** (confirm with the health department) | High-sugar fruit spreads are shelf-stable. |
| Cream cheese frosting or filling | **NOT ALLOWED** | Dairy fillings like cream cheese are treated as potentially hazardous; needs < 41°F. |
| Whipped cream, mousse, pastry cream, custard, curd (lemon curd) | **NOT ALLOWED** | Potentially hazardous; needs refrigeration. |
| Fresh fruit toppings/fillings, cheesecake | **NOT ALLOWED** | Potentially hazardous. |

Default for cupcakes: American buttercream, meringue-powder buttercream, glazes and firm caramel. Get anything in the ASK rows approved by Will County **in writing** before selling.

## 3. Packaging and labels (required on every product once licensed)

Products must be **prepackaged** with a label showing:
1. the name of the cottage food operation and the unit of local government where it's located
2. the **registration number** and the municipality or county where registration was filed
3. the product's common or usual name
4. **all ingredients in descending order by weight**, including colors, artificial flavors and preservatives. For compound ingredients (chocolate chips, sprinkles), list their ingredients too, following federal practice: "chocolate chips (sugar, chocolate, cocoa butter, soy lecithin, vanilla)"
5. the **warning statement**, in prominent lettering, **on the packaging itself**:
   **"This product was produced in a home kitchen not inspected by a health department that may also process common food allergens. If you have safety concerns, contact your local health department."**
6. the **date the product was processed**: full month/day/year per the 2026 addendum
7. **allergens** under federal rules: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts (name the nut, e.g. "pecans"), wheat, peanuts, soybeans and **sesame** (added federally in 2023). Either name them in the ingredient list or add a "Contains:" line after it
8. **net weight**, listed as required in the 2026 addendum

Her **home address is not required** on the label. Labels can be per product *category*, but the health department may ask for more if categories change or labels keep being wrong.

## 4. Where and how she can sell

- **Allowed:** directly to consumers at farmers markets, fairs, festivals and public events; **online**; **pickup at her home** (subject to local rules); **delivery**; pickup at a third party's private property with the owner's consent; mobile farmers markets; pop-ups inside licensed facilities, if clearly separate and she or an employee is present.
- **Shipping:** only foods that don't need refrigeration, **within Illinois only**, with **tamper-evident** sealing (a sticker or seal).
- **Not allowed:** wholesale or resale through bakeries, cafés, grocers or other retailers, or anyone repackaging or reselling her products.
- **Point-of-sale notice:** the same warning statement must be shown prominently where she sells: a **placard** at a physical table, and **a message on the online order page** at the point of sale. Her app's order form will need this warning added before January 2027; remind her.

## 5. Village of Plainfield

The Village requires **all businesses, including home businesses, to register and get a business license** (Ordinance #2083). Contact businesslicensing@goplainfield.com or 815-439-2937. Also ask about home-occupation rules (customer pickups, parking, signage).

## 6. Payments (she has none set up yet)

- **Cash at pickup** works now. Her app also supports pay-first payment links.
- **Payment apps:** use a **business/seller profile** separate from personal use. Most apps require this for selling goods, and it gives purchase protection and clean records. Keep business money separate from personal money; a dedicated free checking account helps a lot at tax time.
- **Card processing fees** reduce margin. Use HER actual fee rate in pricing math; don't assume one.
- **Tax forms:** payment apps send a Form 1099-K only above **$20,000 and more than 200 transactions** in a year (federal threshold restored by the 2025 law, applying to 2025 and 2026). **All business income is taxable whether or not a form is issued.** Some states use lower thresholds.
- **Her policy** of sending the private pickup address only after an order is confirmed and paid is legal and common for home businesses. Pair it with a written refund/cancellation policy on the order form.

## 7. Sales tax (Illinois Department of Revenue)

- Since **January 1, 2026**, Illinois eliminated the 1% **state** tax on "groceries", but **municipalities and counties may impose a local 1% grocery tax** (hundreds did on January 1, 2026, and more on July 1, 2026). The local tax in the RTA region (which includes Will County) was **not** eliminated.
- IDOR lists **"doughnuts, cookies, bagels, rolls or other bakery items"** as **groceries** when sold individually or in a customer-chosen quantity **for consumption off the premises**. Cupcakes and brownies presumably fall under "other bakery items"; confirm with IDOR. Food eaten on site or sold hot is taxed at the higher general rate.
- **What she should do:** check her exact address in IDOR's **Tax Rate Finder**, ask IDOR or a CPA whether she must register (via **MyTax Illinois**) and collect, and keep records of all sales. Never state a specific combined rate without checking her location.

## 8. Business structure (only when she decides)

- **Default: sole proprietor.** No filing is needed to exist; income goes on Schedule C with self-employment tax; keep receipts for ingredients, packaging, labels, equipment and mileage. A CPA can advise on quarterly estimated taxes.
- **Using a business name** ("Flour and Flame Bakery") instead of her own legal name as a sole proprietor: Illinois' **Assumed Business Name Act** requires filing an assumed name certificate with the **County Clerk** of the county where she does business (Will County Clerk). Some counties also require newspaper publication. This is also usually needed to open a bank account in the business name.
- **LLC:** file Articles of Organization (Form LLC-5.5) with the **Illinois Secretary of State**: **$150** filing fee, **$75 annual report**. Get a free **EIN** from the IRS. An LLC separates business and personal liability when kept truly separate, but it doesn't change any cottage food rule. The registration is still for her home kitchen.
- **Insurance:** product liability insurance isn't required by the cottage food law but is strongly recommended. Many markets and events require it.
- **Scaling beyond cottage food** (wholesale to cafés, a storefront): requires a licensed commercial kitchen and a retail food license from the health department, and cottage products can't be made there.
