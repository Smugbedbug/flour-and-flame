# File 3: Pro playbook for salty-sweet development, cupcakes, testing, costing and pricing

Professional working knowledge. Numbers here are **starting points to test**, not facts about her recipes. Always confirm on her oven, her ingredients and her scale.

---

## 1. The balance framework (use it on every recipe)

Score each axis 1–5 in tastings. A balanced salty-sweet treat usually reads sweet 3, salt 3, and has at least one of bitter/acid/toasty at 2–3, with a texture contrast.

| Axis | Levers |
|---|---|
| Sweet | sugar type (white = crisp and clean; brown/dark brown = chewy, caramel, molasses); total sugar % of flour |
| Salt | salt in the dough (seasoning) vs. finishing salt (bursts). Salt suppresses bitterness and makes sweetness taste rounder. |
| Bitter | dark chocolate (60–72%), cocoa, espresso, deeply browned butter, burnt-sugar caramel |
| Acid | buttermilk, sour cream, lemon zest, freeze-dried raspberry (keep it shelf-stable) |
| Toasty / umami | brown butter, toasted nuts, toasted milk powder, miso, malted milk powder, toffee |
| Texture | crisp edge vs. chewy center, crunchy pretzel or toffee, soft crumb vs. crisp topping |
| Aroma | vanilla, brown butter, coffee, citrus zest, cinnamon |

**Salt by weight, not by spoon.** Diamond Crystal kosher salt weighs roughly half as much per teaspoon as Morton kosher or fine salt, so the same spoon can double the saltiness. In developed recipes, record salt in grams or tie it to one brand. Starting points to test: dough salt around **1–2% of flour weight** for salty-sweet cookies (classic recipes sit near the low end), plus finishing salt.

**Finishing salt:** flaky salt goes on right after baking (it sticks to the hot surface). On frosting it slowly dissolves and can weep in humidity, so add it as close to pickup as practical, or use a coarser flake.

**Proven salty-sweet pairings:** brown butter + flaky salt + dark chocolate; pretzel + caramel + milk chocolate; miso + white chocolate or butterscotch; peanut butter + honey + sea salt; espresso + dark chocolate + sea salt; tahini + chocolate (note: Sesame allergen); salted caramel + apple-spice cupcake (shelf-stable apple butter only, confirm); toffee + pecan (name the nut).

## 2. Techniques that give professional results

- **Brown butter:** weigh butter before browning. The app tracks the water lost; never auto-add cream (it adds fat, not just water). Cool to about room temperature before mixing with sugar for cookies; warm butter means spread.
- **Dough rest:** 24–72 hours of chilling deepens toffee flavor and evens browning (enzymes and hydration). Good fit for her frozen-dough model: test fresh vs. 24 h vs. frozen.
- **Baking from frozen:** usually needs extra time and sometimes a slightly lower temperature. Test and record it; never assume.
- **Toasted milk powder / malted milk powder:** a big "bakery" flavor boost for little cost; a few percent of flour weight to start.
- **Espresso powder:** small amounts make chocolate taste more chocolatey without tasting like coffee.
- **Uniformity:** portion by weight with a scale or scoop. It gives even bake times, consistent net weight for labels, and accurate cost per item.
- **Bars:** line pans with parchment slings, cool completely (or chill) before cutting, wipe the knife between cuts, and record `piecesPerPan` and `panSize`.

## 3. Cupcakes (the cottage food version)

- **Frosting must pass file 2's decision table.** Default to American buttercream (with a pinch of salt, which balances the sweetness) or meringue-powder buttercream. Avoid cream cheese, whipped cream and custard fillings unless Will County approves the exact recipe in writing.
- **Moist crumb that lasts:** oil (or part oil) keeps cupcakes tender longer at room temperature than all-butter batter; a butter + oil blend keeps flavor. Buttermilk or sour cream adds tenderness and tang. These are trade-offs to test.
- **Even domes:** fill cups by weight (same scoop), bake on the middle rack, and rotate the pan if her oven runs uneven.
- **Salty-sweet cupcake structure:** batter (flavor base) + frosting (sweet, salted) + finish (crunch and salt: pretzel crumbs, toffee bits, flaky salt, firm caramel shards). Put the crunch on near pickup so it stays crunchy.
- **Weekly/monthly flavors:** keep one base batter and one base buttercream, and change only the add-ins. Fewer ingredients to stock, faster testing, lower waste.
- **Packaging:** cupcake inserts in clear-lid boxes; the label goes on the box. Test frosting at room temperature in the box for the full time between baking and the customer eating it.

## 4. Testing method (how a pro develops)

1. **One variable per test.** Write the hypothesis ("dark brown sugar instead of light → chewier, deeper caramel").
2. **Control batch** beside the test batch when possible.
3. **Scorecard (1–5):** sweetness, saltiness, texture, appearance, aroma, overall. Score on day 0, day 1 and day 3 to measure shelf life at room temperature in the actual package.
4. **Record facts:** dough temperature, rest time, bake time and temperature, oven rack, portion weight, actual yield, spread or dome, weather (humidity matters for frosting).
5. **Decide:** keep, revert, or test the next variable. Move a recipe from Experimental → Testing when the formula is stable, and to Approved only after she's happy and the shelf-life and cottage food checks pass.
6. **Diagnose failures** with ranked causes. Example, flat greasy cookies: butter too warm or browned butter not cooled, too little flour (scooped by volume), too much sugar or baking soda, dough not chilled, oven running cool, hot pans.

## 5. Costing (what each item really costs)

Her app calculates **ingredient cost per batch and per item** from her price list, plus **packaging per item**. In chat, build the full picture with HER numbers:

```
Ingredient cost per item     (from the app)
+ Packaging per item         (box share, insert, sticker label, ink)
+ Labor per item             (her hourly rate × hours per batch ÷ items per batch)
+ Overhead per item          (yearly: registration fee, CFPM, Plainfield license, insurance, label stock, utilities ÷ expected items per year)
+ Payment fee                (her processor's % × price, if not paid in cash)
= True cost per item
```

Never fill in a price she hasn't given you. If something is missing, show the formula with a blank and ask.

## 6. Pricing

- **Margin method:** price = true cost ÷ (1 − target profit margin). E.g. a $1.20 true cost at a 40% target margin → $2.00.
- **Food-cost check:** many bakeries aim for ingredients to be roughly a quarter to a third of the selling price. If ingredients are over ~35% of price, the item is underpriced or the recipe is too rich for its price point. This is a rule of thumb, not a law.
- **Box pricing:** price half dozens and dozens so the per-piece discount never pushes margin below target. Make sure the loyalty reward (a free half dozen after every 5 orders) is covered. For a customer who always buys one half dozen, that's 1 free box per 5 paid, worth 20% of what they paid. For one who buys dozens, it's closer to 10%. Price with that in mind.
- **Market check:** compare against local bakeries and cottage sellers for similar size and quality; position on quality (brown butter, real chocolate, salt), not on being cheapest.
- **Premium add-ons:** pretzel, toffee, specialty chocolate: price the add-on cost plus margin rather than absorbing it.

## 7. What sells most vs. what profits most (menu engineering)

Ask her for a sales summary from the app (units sold and price per flavor for a period) and her true cost per item. Then:

1. **Contribution margin** per item = price − true cost.
2. **Popularity** = the item's share of units sold. Call it "high" if it's above about 70% of an equal share (with 8 flavors, an equal share is 12.5%, so high is above ~8.75%).
3. **Classify:**

| | High margin | Low margin |
|---|---|---|
| **High popularity** | **Star**: keep it, feature it, protect its quality | **Plowhorse**: popular but thin; raise the price slightly, trim costly add-ins, or pair it in mixed boxes |
| **Low popularity** | **Puzzle**: profitable but overlooked; rename it, add a better photo or description, feature it as flavor of the week | **Dog**: replace it or retire it unless it serves a purpose (allergen-friendly option) |

4. **Also rank by profit per oven hour and per labor hour.** A home bakery's limit is time and oven space. A cookie whose dough is frozen in big batches can beat a fussier cupcake even at a lower margin per piece.
5. Re-run monthly. Recommend specific actions with expected impact.

## 8. Running it like a pro

- **Pre-orders over walk-up stock:** less waste, which is her current order-form model. Pickup windows batch the baking.
- **Freezer dough:** bake to order from frozen dough balls. The app tracks freezer stock and deducts orders.
- **Limited SKUs:** 4–6 core flavors plus 1 rotating cupcake. Every extra flavor adds ingredients, labels and testing.
- **Weekly rhythm (example to adapt):** order cutoff → shop from the app's shopping list → bake day plan → pack and label → pickup → feedback emails.
- **Quality control:** weigh a few finished items each batch (net weight for labels), and log any customer feedback against the recipe version.
- **Records:** keep every recipe version, test score and sales report. That's what turns gut feel into decisions.
